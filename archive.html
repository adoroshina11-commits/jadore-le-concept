Enter the Archive
001
Maison Margiela

Tabi Boot

1989
Recognition
Form
Anti-luxury.

What began as a radical split-toe silhouette became one of fashion’s most recognizable objects. I love the Tabi because its identity does not depend on a visible logo. The shape itself became the house code.

Form   ·   Identity   ·   Anti-Luxury
002
Christian Dior

Saddle Bag

1999

Few handbags reject conventional shape as confidently as the Saddle. Its asymmetry turned an everyday object into a silhouette recognizable from across a room — part accessory, part symbol, part piece of fashion history.

Galliano   ·   Icon   ·   Reinvention
003
Alexander McQueen

Armadillo Boot

2009

The Armadillo boot sits somewhere between footwear, sculpture and creature. Its proportions deliberately disturb the familiar shape of the human body — exactly the kind of object that makes fashion capable of feeling strange again.

Sculpture   ·   Fantasy   ·   Transformation
004
Thierry Mugler

Chimère

1997

More creature than conventional couture, Chimère represents the point at which clothing becomes mythology. The body is not merely dressed; it is transformed into something imagined.

Couture   ·   Mythology   ·   Transformation
005
Schiaparelli

Surrealist Object

Archive

Schiaparelli has always treated fashion as a place where the ordinary can become absurd, theatrical or surreal. I am drawn to objects that make you look twice — pieces that behave almost like visual jokes while remaining extraordinarily crafted.

Surrealism   ·   Art   ·   Provocation
006
Comme des Garçons

Body Meets Dress

1997

Rei Kawakubo challenged one of fashion’s most basic assumptions: that clothing should flatter the existing body. By inserting unexpected volume, the collection asked whether fashion could invent an entirely different silhouette instead.

Body   ·   Distortion   ·   Radical Form
007
Hermès

Birkin

1984

The Birkin is interesting to me not simply because it is expensive, but because an ordinary functional object became surrounded by an extraordinary cultural mythology of access, scarcity, collecting and status.

Scarcity   ·   Status   ·   Myth
008
Cartier

An Object in Motion

Archive

Jewelry becomes especially fascinating when engineering disappears beneath beauty. Cartier’s transformable pieces reveal how an object can carry multiple identities while still feeling completely resolved.

Jewelry   ·   Mechanism   ·   Transformation
009
Guy Bourdin

The Image

Archive

Fashion does not survive only through garments. Sometimes the photograph becomes the object we remember. Bourdin’s images prove how framing, colour, tension and atmosphere can become as recognizable as the fashion itself.

Image   ·   Desire   ·   Memory
